Go is my main language — about three years daily across FlowPay and Orbit. I'm
comfortable and productive in it; solid, not a language expert.

What I'm good at: clear idiomatic services, the standard concurrency patterns
(channels, worker pools, context for cancellation and deadlines), table-driven
tests, and reaching for pprof when something's slow. I've debugged real goroutine
leaks and a couple of nasty data races with the race detector.

Where I'm still growing: I lean on well-trodden patterns and I'm not the person
who's going to invent a clever generic abstraction — and honestly I think that's
fine for the code I write. I've read enough of the runtime to reason about the
scheduler and GC at a high level, but I couldn't give a deep talk on it.

My honest take: Go rewards boring, and boring is most of what production needs. It
suits how I work.
