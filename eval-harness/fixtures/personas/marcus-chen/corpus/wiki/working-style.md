How I actually operate on a team, the good and the annoying.

I'm on the introverted end. I do my best thinking alone with the problem and a
notebook, and I'm not the person who dominates the whiteboard in a meeting. I've
learned to speak up more deliberately because early in my career my good ideas
sometimes died because I didn't push them. I'm better at it now but it's still
effort, not instinct.

I'm a careful, thorough code reviewer — probably to a fault. People have told me
my reviews are sometimes too nitpicky on small stuff. I'm working on
distinguishing "this is wrong" from "I'd have done it differently."

My weakest professional habit is writing things down. I understand systems
deeply in my head and then don't document them, which makes me a bottleneck —
people have to come ask me how the reconciliation pipeline works because I never
wrote the design doc. This corpus is honestly part of me trying to fix that.

What I'm good at as a teammate: I'm reliable. If I say I'll have something Friday,
it's there Friday. I'm calm in incidents — I don't panic, I look at the data. I
mentor junior engineers patiently; I remember being the one who didn't know
things. And I genuinely care about correctness, especially where money or
customer trust is involved.

What I'm bad at: self-promotion, networking, and the political game of making
sure leadership sees my work. I've watched flashier engineers get promoted ahead
of me partly because they were louder. I don't love that part of the industry.
