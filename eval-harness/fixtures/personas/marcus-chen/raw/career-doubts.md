can't sleep, writing this down.

the doubt that gets me: am I actually good or am I just experienced. there's a
difference. I've been doing this six years so I'm fast and I don't make rookie
mistakes, but when I'm in a room with someone from a strong background I can feel
that they think about problems at a level I don't. they reach for the right
abstraction immediately. I get there but slower and sometimes I don't get there
at all, I just ship the thing that works.

is "ships things that work" enough? for a lot of jobs yes. for the jobs I
actually want, I'm not sure.

the impostor thing cuts both ways though. I've also watched people who interview
great and talk a big game write worse code than me and create messes I have to
clean up. so maybe the gap I feel is partly in my head. hard to calibrate.

what I'm trying to do about it: reading more deeply (working through DDIA again,
actually doing the exercises this time), taking on the hardest thing available at
work even when it's scary, and interviewing at places that are a stretch so I get
honest signal about where I actually stand.

not sharing this with interviewers obviously, but it's the real internal monologue
behind "I want more growth." it's not a tidy career-narrative thing, it's "I'm
afraid of becoming a guy who peaked at mid-level and called it experience."
