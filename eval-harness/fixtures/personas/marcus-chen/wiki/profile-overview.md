I'm Marcus Chen, a backend engineer with about six years of experience, mostly
in Java/Spring early on and Go for the last three years. I've worked at three
companies: a mid-size e-commerce shop (ACME Retail), a fintech startup
(FlowPay), and a B2B SaaS company (Orbit) where I am now.

I'd describe myself as a steady, get-it-done engineer rather than a 10x rockstar.
I'm good at taking a vaguely specified business problem, breaking it into
services and database tables, and shipping something that works and doesn't fall
over. I've owned order systems, a payment reconciliation pipeline, and a
notification service. I'm comfortable on call.

Where I'm honest about my limits: I haven't operated anything at truly massive
scale — my biggest system did maybe 2,000 requests per second at peak, not
hundreds of thousands. My algorithms and CS-theory chops are average; I can do a
medium LeetCode problem but I'd be lying if I said I love them. My system-design
interviews are hit or miss because I tend to over-explain the parts I know and
hand-wave the parts I don't (caching invalidation, consensus, that kind of
thing).

I think a lot about how systems fail and how teams actually ship, and I'm not
great at writing it down — which is partly why this corpus exists. I'm looking
for a role with more technical depth than my current one.
